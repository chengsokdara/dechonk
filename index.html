How it works

Three steps. Zero regret.

01

Scan

A parallel Rust walker sweeps your home folder, all drives, or anywhere you point it. Nested installs are deduplicated and junk folders are skipped before they can waste a millisecond.

02

Sizes stream in live as it walks. Sort by chonk, filter by how long nobody has touched it, and deal with the biggest offenders first.

Dechonk

Safety

What Dechonk will never do

Touch your source files

Only dependency folders are targets. Your code isn't even scanned, let alone moved.

Follow symlinks into the void

Symlinks are never followed. Whatever lives outside the folder stays outside the folder.

Delete without asking

Everything stops at the event horizon first: your OS Trash, fully recoverable. The singularity (permanent delete) always asks, and always says exactly what it will free.

Phone home

No telemetry, no accounts, no network. The scanner doesn't know what the internet is.

FAQ

Questions from the event horizon

Is deleting node_modules safe?+

Yes. Your source code is never touched, and everything in node_modules is regenerable with npm install (or pnpm/yarn/bun; Dechonk doesn't judge). It's the only multi-gigabyte folder on your machine you're supposed to delete.

Does Dechonk delete anything permanently?+

No. Folders always stop at the event horizon first (your OS Trash, fully recoverable). Dropping them into the singularity (permanent deletion) is opt-in and always behind a confirmation dialog that tells you exactly how much space it frees.

How fast is the scan?+

The walker is written in Rust and runs discovery and size enrichment in parallel. Whole-drive scans typically finish in seconds, and you watch the numbers land live while it works.

Which platforms does it support?+

macOS, Windows, and Linux. The app is built with Tauri, weighs about 4 MB, and opens instantly. Free, no account required.

Why is it called Dechonk?+

Because the internet decided oversized folders are "chonky," and we are not strong enough to fight the internet. Dechonking is the act of restoring a folder to a reasonable mass. See also: the chonk scale.
